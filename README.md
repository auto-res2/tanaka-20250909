# FlashGAT: Unified Importance-Driven Sparsification and IO-Aware Attention for Billion-Edge Graphs
> ⚠️ **NOTE:** This research is an automatic research using AIRAS.
## Abstract
Graph Attention Networks (GATs) excel on small graphs because they learn per-edge importance, yet they remain impractical for web-scale deployments. Three bottlenecks dominate: (i) quadratic attention cost in high-degree neighbourhoods, (ii) very low GPU arithmetic intensity due to irregular scatter–gather, and (iii) CPU-side neighbour sampling that materialises full adjacency before discarding most edges. Prior work addresses either the statistical side with variance-reduced samplers [zeng-2019-graphsaint, liu-2020-bandit] or the systems side with IO-aware kernels such as FlashAttention [dao-2022-flashattention], but never both jointly. We introduce FlashGAT, an end-to-end training pipeline that closes this gap through five components: a lightweight 8-bit rolling edge-importance preview, a bandit-guided Top-B filter equipped with control-variates, GPU-resident block repacking, a half-precision block-sparse FlashAttention kernel, and balanced-orthogonal initialisation plus GACT activation compression [liu-2022-gact, mustafa-2023-are]. On four OGB benchmarks and synthetic graphs with one billion edges, FlashGAT matches full-neighbour accuracy while accelerating training by 3–8×, reducing peak memory by 2–3×, and raising GPU utilisation from 18 % to 63 %. Comprehensive ablations confirm that the new Pareto frontier emerges only from the joint design of sampling, layout, and kernel.
